You draw the motion. It writes the math.

Flick is an Android app for motion design. Drag the way an animation should feel. It fits that gesture to an easing curve and hands you the code.

Easing tools usually start from two handles on a graph. Flick starts from the gesture. Hesitate, dash, overshoot, and the shape of what you did becomes the curve.

What one flick does

The phone reads historical touch samples, not just the delivered event, then smooths them with a One Euro filter. Progress is the projection onto the start-to-end line, so a pull-back is negative and anticipation shows up.

Three models compete. A cubic bezier is preferred when it is good enough. A spring is chosen when you overshoot and settle. A path of cubic Hermite segments, with knots on the velocity extrema, is used when the motion has real beats. You can then drag the bezier handles, snap them, mirror them, clamp overshoot, and nudge the duration.

The result is named in animator language: anticipation, overshoot and settle, front-loaded or back-loaded. Hold Feel and the curve plays as vibration. Save curves with names and tags. The library exports as one Motion.kt.

Stack

Java, on the Android SDK, with no Gradle and no third-party libraries. build.sh runs aapt2, javac, d8 and apksigner. The math package has no Android imports and is tested on a desktop JVM.

Exports: CSS, CSS linear(), Jetpack Compose (CubicBezierEasing or spring()), Android pathInterpolator, Flutter, SwiftUI, and After Effects keyframe influence.
