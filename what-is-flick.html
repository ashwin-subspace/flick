The inversion

Handles on a graph are the wrong input

Open cubic-bezier.com, the Compose docs, or the After Effects graph editor and you get the same interface: two control handles you nudge until the preview stops looking wrong. You already know how the movement should feel. The tool makes you translate that feeling into coordinates by trial and error.

Flick removes the translation step. The gesture is the input. Hesitate, then dash, then let it settle — and the shape of what you did becomes the shape of the curve.

Inside one flick

Four stages, in order

Each stage feeds the next, so a single drag arrives as pasteable code.

CAPTURE

Read the whole gesture

Android batches touch input — read only the delivered event and most of the panel's 120–240 Hz resolution is thrown away. Flick reads the historical samples too, then smooths with a One Euro filter, which cleans up hesitation without dulling a dash.

FIT

Three models compete

Progress is measured by projecting your finger onto the start-to-end line, so a pull-back reads as negative progress. A bezier, a spring and a cubic spline each try to explain the trace. The simplest honest one wins.

READ

Name what you did

It reports the motion in an animator's language rather than raw numbers: how deep the anticipation was, how far it overshot and how long it took to settle, whether the energy sits at the front or the back.

EXPORT

Hand over the code

Seven targets, duration included — because a feel is a shape and a length. Save the curve with tags, and the whole library exports as one Motion.kt.

The fit

A bezier cannot bounce

This is the part most easing tools skip. A single cubic bezier can't overshoot and settle, and it can't hesitate twice — so a tool that only offers beziers quietly forces every motion into a shape it may not have. Flick fits three models and tells you which one your gesture actually is.

Model Params Fitted with Chosen when
Bezier 4 Nelder-Mead from 6 starts, Newton solve to invert x(t) It lands within 1.8% — the common case, and what most people want
Spring 2 Damped harmonic step response, fits damping ratio and frequency You overshot and settled, and a spring explains it as well as a bezier
Path 2 per knot Cubic Hermite spline, knots on the velocity extrema Neither fits — the motion has real beats and needs a chain of cubics

Selection is rule-based rather than pure lowest-error, because a many-knot spline nearly always wins on raw error while being the least useful answer. On one test gesture — hesitate, dash, overshoot, settle — the path fit at 0.014 error where the bezier managed 0.090. That's when the extra complexity is earned.

Judging it

Five things move at once

One moving box is a lie. A curve that feels perfect on translation feels sluggish on opacity and wrong on scale — so the preview drives a box, a scaling circle, a fading card, a rotation and a staggered list together, with a faint ghost running the platform default in sync behind each one. Scrub through it, slow it to a quarter speed, or hold Feel and the curve plays as vibration, amplitude following velocity, so you can judge the shape with the screen off.

That After Effects block is the one nobody has in their pocket. AE doesn't use control points — it uses influence, the percentage of the time span each handle occupies. Converting between the two is an annoying bit of arithmetic that sits exactly on the seam between writing code and doing motion design.
